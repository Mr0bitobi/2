# Reproducible Research Workflow

## 1. Scope the question

Record the exact question, time period, version/continuity, required output, and what would count as a satisfactory answer.

## 2. Retrieve and rank evidence

Prefer primary records and original materials when they directly answer the question. Use official documentation and statements for what an organization or creator explicitly published. Use reputable independent sources for context and corroboration. Community discussions are useful discovery leads but should not silently become proof.

## 3. Keep a claim ledger

For each material claim, record the claim text, source URL, exact page/section/chapter/episode, publication or update date where available, evidence excerpt or faithful paraphrase, what it establishes, what it does not establish, confidence, and competing evidence.

## 4. Separate epistemic states

- Confirmed: directly supported by adequate evidence.
- Supported inference: reasoned conclusion not directly stated.
- Disputed: credible evidence conflicts or relevant versions differ.
- Unknown: evidence is insufficient.
- Speculation: hypothetical extrapolation.

Do not convert missing evidence into proof of impossibility. Do not treat copies of one source as independent corroboration.

## 5. Test against alternatives

Look for contradictions, relevant counterexamples, alternate definitions, changing versions, and evidence that could falsify the initial hypothesis. Treat search snippets as pointers, not final proof when the underlying source is available.

## 6. Write and audit

Answer directly. Support material factual claims with citations. Explain assumptions and limitations. Check that each source supports its attached claim, dates and versions are appropriate, and the conclusion is no stronger than the evidence permits.

## Fictional ability analysis

Treat each ability as a constrained record: demonstrated effect, prerequisite, activation, target, range, duration, cost, limits, counters, failure conditions, continuity, and source. Keep destructive output, speed, perception, durability, regeneration, adaptation, sealing, mental effects, spatial effects, and tactical skill distinct unless the source establishes an interaction. A feat does not establish unlimited capability. Resistance to one effect does not establish universal immunity. Cross-system interaction must be supported or labeled as an assumption.
